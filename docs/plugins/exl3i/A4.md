**Verdict: the gate passes, narrowly for the exact-enough fold under the plugin's own flags.** The A4 tile with the g64
activation-scale fold is ×1.24–1.38 faster than the A8 tile per big shape at M 2048 in the slowest exact-enough variant

**The tile** (`exl3i_4a4.hip.h`): the A8 tile (`exl3i_8a8.hip.h`, radiance's i8a8 prefill tile with EXL3's store) at
the byte level, unchanged (block 128 × 128, 8 waves 4 × 2, 2 × 4 tiles a wave, weight straight from a fragment-order
plane into registers, activation staged through LDS, same epilogue); each byte holds two inputs, so a 64-byte stage is
128 inputs and the tile issues `v_wmma_i32_16x16x32_iu4` instead of iu8. Per 64 inputs (2 WMMA steps) the int32 group
sums are folded into f32 accumulators with the (row, group) activation scale. EXL3's row scales (svh), the output
Hadamard and WS stay in the store as in A8. **The i4 grid is half-integer** (levels (q + 1/2) · st, q ∈ −8..7,
st = 2.6 / 7.5; quantize/exl3-patches 0006): the plane stores q (int4 exactly), and the 1/2 · Σx term, the same for
every output row, is added after the output Hadamard at each 128-block's row 0 (+64 · R[m], R = Σ_g scale_g · Σ_{k∈g} q_a,
written by the activation quantiser). One FMA per row and block; exact in the test.

**What limits the fold.** On gfx1201 the fold's VALU work does not overlap the WMMAs: the no-fold tile runs at
400–450 TOPS, and every fold FMA a slot a group costs about what its instruction count predicts (one FMA a slot a group
= 128 VOPD pairs a 64-WMMA stage pair; +35% over no fold). The compiler already dual-issues the fold
(`v_dual_fmac_f32`), so with float group scales one op a slot a group is the floor; g64 → g128 halves it (×1.55 vs ×1.32
on the layer). Exact float folds lose to the biased one despite the same op count: the subtract / convert sits between
the WMMA result and the FMA (a dependent chain the scheduler does not hide; g64s / g64c ×1.05–1.20). Two sets of group
sums (fold of one group while the next one's WMMAs run) at 5 waves a SIMD: slower (×1.14–1.24; the compiler
re-serialised it at the same VGPR count). g64b2 spills (300 B / lane) under this compiler: avoid. Register use: g64b1
219 VGPRs, no spill, occupancy 6 (the A8 tile: 240 + 20 B / lane spill).

**Recommendation for step 2:** ship the g64b1 fold as A4-g64 (the exact-enough one), keep g64b4 as a build option
(+6% layer speed for ~1.5–3% more activation noise power), and give #46-03 the g128 number: A4-g128 is ×1.55 vs A8 on
the layer against ×1.32 for g64, so if its KLD cost is close to g64's (E1b on K6: gate_up g128 +0.009 vs g64 +0.008),
g128 may be the better knapsack option. These are tile-only numbers: the A4 path adds the activation quantiser and the
decode pass (in A8: actq 8%, wdec8 11% of the layer; the int4 plane halves wdec's write bytes).

`local/workers/instr3/w34-results/` (r3 checks, r5 M 2048, r6 sweep).

## Step 2: the A4 path (w34, 2026-11-05)

### Contract changes

- **Codebook `mul1i4`** (exl3i oracle `common/exl3i_decode.h`, `EXL3I_CB_MUL1I4 = 2`; encoding `"mul1i4/h128/k<rate>"`):
  the fork's int4 grid (quantize/exl3-patches 0006, `EXL3_MUL1_I4`), q = clamp(rint(f32(mul1 value) · INV4 − ½), −8, 7),
  value fp16(f32(q + ½) · ST4), ST4 = f32(2.6f / 7.5f), INV4 = f32(7.5f / 2.6f). Fused or unfused `v · INV4 − ½` gives
  the same q for every state (checked), so nvcc's contraction in the quantizer does not matter. **The values are
  half-integers on the grid: they do not fit int4 as values.** The int4 plane stores q (−8..7, exact), and A4 adds the
  ½ · Σx term per row (R, from the activation quantiser) after the output Hadamard. A4 multiplies the ideal grid
  (q + ½)·ST4; the fp16 values the quantizer and the f16 path use differ from it by at most 0.0017 of a step.
  **Importer (branch qc-exl3-v3-i4imp, w53):** `exl3quant`'s `exl3i` takes `codebook=mul1i4` and checks it against
  ourq's `stamp.json` (`--codebook i4`); `v3/infer/ref/exl3i_ref.c` learns mul1i4 from a rad-exl3-mix patch (the host
  test cross-checks all three codebooks once it is applied, two before).
- **Act modes `a4g<N>`** (`include/exl3_act.h`, kind `EXL3_ACT_A4G`, N a multiple of 64): metadata
  `exl3.act.<weight> = a4g64` (or `a4g128`). Rows: `exl3i_gemm_nt_a4` takes `a4g64 a4g128` on `mul1i4` only (K % 256).
  exl3r4d / exl3dense list only `f16` for act, so a V1 / V2 weight never takes an A4 mode.
- **A8 on `mul1i4`**: `exl3i_gemm_nt_a8` takes `mul1i8 mul1i4`. mul1i4's int8 plane index is 2q + 1 and its step
  ST4 / 2 (the A8 GEMM's store constant follows the codebook). The f16 row takes `mul1 mul1i8 mul1i4`.
- Versions: `EXL3I_IFACE_VERSION`, `EXL3I_ENCODING_VERSION`, `EXL3I_DECODE_API_VERSION` 1 → 2; plugin 0.3.0.
- **Shared-input groups** (qwen35_exl3): attn qg / k / v and ssm qkv / z must carry one act mode; a container whose
  metadata gives a group two modes is refused at declare (`exl3_act_same`), by name. gate_up is one weight already.
  qwen35_exl3_test updated (its ssm group now has one mode) plus a refusal case.

### What runs (a weight in `mul1i4` with act `a4g64` at M ≥ 512 real rows; below, the f16 path's nt_pass)

1. **actq4** (`exl3i_a4_prep.hip`): one wave per (plane, row). Each 128-feature block is rotated by `rotate128_math`
   (the f16 path's values, bit for bit), then quantised with scale = amax / 7 and q = rint(clamp(v · 7 / amax, ±7)) per
   64 inputs (a 16-lane row of the wave). Outputs: the int4 row (nibble order below), the f32 scales, and
   R = Σ_g f32(scale_g · Σq) summed in group order.
2. **wdec4** (same file): one wave per (16-row tile, 128-input supertile). `decode4` (RateInt / RateFrac) turns each lane's
   8 values of a trellis tile into one dword of int4 indices with the closed form n = q + 8 =
   clamp((5S − 504) >> 8, 0, 15) from the byte sum S (four packed ops), packed in the decoder's own element order. One
   dword is exchanged with lane ^ 16 per WMMA step (the same exchange as wdec8), and each lane stores its 32 bytes whole.
3. **gemm** (`exl3i_a4_gemm.hip`): the step-1 tile, `kFoldBias` with `EXL3I_A4_RBF` (default 1; reported by
   `exl3i_a4_rbf()`), g64 or g128 instances; + 64·R at each block's row 0; C = ST4 / √128.

The nibble order (`exl3i_a4.h`): within each 16-input block, fragment position p holds input
`exl3i_i4_kl(p)` = 0 2 8 10 1 3 9 11 | 4 6 12 14 5 7 13 15. This is the decoder's element order, so the weight packs
without shuffles; actq4 writes the rows the same way, and lane pairs merge their nibbles with one quad_perm DPP.

Arena (`exl3i_a4_plan`): int4 rows [mats][M][K/2], scales [mats][M][K/G], R [mats][M], one chunk's int4 plane
(K/2 bytes a column). At 4096 rows: gate_up 107.5 MB (A8: 211.2), down 80.8 MB (A8: 155.1).

### What limits it, and the README's rule

- **The fold**: one VALU op per output slot per 64 inputs, not overlapped with the WMMA on gfx1201. That costs +35–45%
  over the no-fold tile. Exact float folds are slower still (the convert / subtract sits on the WMMA's result). VOPD
  pairing (patched LLVM 24 only) is worth ~5% of the layer; g128 halves the fold (×1.51 vs A8 instead of ×1.31).
  Faster g64 options are recorded in step 1: RBF 4 (×1.35–1.40, +3–5% of A4's error power) and integer scales
  (exact fold, its own scale-format error).
- **Outside the GEMM**: actq4 + wdec4 are 18% of the A4 path. actq4 runs one wave a row (R's order), at A8 actq's speed.
  A shared actq for attention q / k / v and GDN qkv / z (one input, one mode) would save ~0.2 ms of the layer mean.
- **The rule** (≥ 10% lower predicted gemm_us at equal predicted KLD, or ≥ 0.03 bpw smaller): A4 g64 takes ~23% off
  each tensor's A8 time, so the knapsack needs A4 on ≥ ~43% of the A8 layer time to save 10% overall.
  - gate_up alone is 46% of it (one weight: one mode): ~10.3%. gate_up + down: ~17%.
  - With g128 (−34% a tensor): gate_up alone ~16%.
  - **Reachable in speed if #46-03's costs let A4 onto gate_up (or gate_up + down) at ≤ 3 bpw** at a KLD the
    knapsack can buy back. Whether it can is #46-03 step 1's call. Price A4-g128 as well: it is 15% faster than g64
    on the layer.
