# #94 rerun · int8-rounded codebook above K7 on w43's exllamav3-faithful proxy (w45, 2026-11-06)

Companion to `README.md` "#94" (question, rule and sanity check unchanged; not edited). #94's first run
(`trellis2.py`, scale grid to 1.15) failed its sanity check: K7 ×1.18 / K8 ×3.0 against the GPU smoke's ×1.024 / ×1.347.
The grid-to-1.5 rerun (`i8hi-g15.jsonl`) gave ×1.29 / ×4.53, still "OFF". This rerun uses w43's proxy v2
(`v3/e3/settle/settle.py`: exllamav3's regularize transforms, its g_scale search done with each arm's own codebook,
`--scaled-hessian` LDLQ, the per-output-channel scale refit).
**Runtime cost:** none (no i8 level is added; same format and kernels).

## Result

**Step 1 (sanity) passes, and it explains the old failure.** On the GPU smoke's own tensors (`cbsmoke.py`: 4,096
Gaussian 256-value tiles from rng 11, sd 1, fed directly to the trellis with no scale), settle.py's trellis and
codebook core gives int8 / mul1 = **×1.028 at K7 and ×1.362 at K8** (free start), and ×1.022 / ×1.357 with exllamav3's
tail-biting search, against the GPU's ×1.024 / ×1.347: within ±0.05. The exact exllamav3 codebook values give
×1.031 / ×1.359. fp16 search arithmetic changes nothing (×1.027 / ×1.356).
The ratio depends almost entirely on the tile scale:

| tile sd fed to the trellis | 0.8 | 0.9 | **1 (the smoke)** | 1.1 | 1.25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K7 int8 / mul1 | 1.31 | 1.14 | **1.03** | 1.00 | 0.99 |
| K8 int8 / mul1 | 4.54 | 2.46 | **1.36** | 1.08 | 1.00 |

At sd 1 the codebook's ±3.4 sd range clips the Gaussian tails. That overload error is shared by both codebooks and
swamps the int8 grid's rounding error: mul1's K8 MSE is 1.17e-4 at sd 1 against 2.25e-5 at sd 0.8. The real
quantizer never runs there. exllamav3's search picks g_scale ≈ 0.57–0.67 at K6.5–8 (tile RMS ≈ 0.71–0.83 codebook sd),
where rounding dominates. **The GPU smoke measured the i8 cost under a condition the quantizer avoids.** #94's sanity
check compared the proxy against that number. Both CPU proxies (old trellis2 with the wider grid, and settle.py) agree
with each other and with E3b (×1.25 / ×4.5).

**Verdict: no i8 level K7.25 / 7.5 / 7.75 joins the ladder**, by a wide margin (the closest, K7.25, sits at ×1.69). All
the checks agree:
- sample 1: K7 ×1.268, K7.25 ×1.686;

Tensors differ little: attn_qg 19 is the worst at every K (×1.39 at K7) and attn_output 19 the best.

**What an i8 level is worth** (the mul1 K with the same proxy error, per tensor, log-interpolated on mul1's curve):

| i8 level | ≈ mul1 K | bits lost | error vs i8 K7 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 6.5 | 6.49 | 0.01 | ×1.53 |
| 7 | 6.82 (6.74–6.83) | **0.18** | 1 |
| 7.25 | 6.85 | 0.40 | ×0.96 |
| 7.5 | 6.85 | 0.65 | ×0.95 |
| 8 | 6.86 | 1.14 | ×0.95 |

The int8 grid has a floor at ≈ mul1 K6.85. Above K7 an i8 level buys at most 5% less error for up to a whole bit.

## For the lead (not decided here)
1. **i8 K7 is not free.** The plan's "×1.02 at K7" comes from the smoke. On real tensors at the quantizer's own scale,
   the proxy says ×1.27 (≈ 0.18 bit; the attn_qg tensor 0.26 bit). i8 at K6.5 is ×1.018 (0.01 bit).
   Any size that puts K ≥ 7 tensors on the int8 path should price i8 K7 as ≈ mul1 K6.8 in the knapsack, or keep
   them on mul1 / f16.
2. **Suggested confirmation:** replace the Gaussian-tile smoke as the reference with a real measurement:
   `ourq.py --codebook i8` vs mul1 at K6.5 / 7 / 8 on 1–2 27B tensors, rel under H_in. That is a few minutes on the
   4070. `cbsmoke.py`'s own ratio check would also need scaled tiles (×0.8) to mean anything at K ≥ 7.
3. #94's INDEX row and a LOG entry still need writing (I did not edit them). Status: done, negative; the sanity
   failure is explained (the smoke ran at a scale the quantizer avoids).
